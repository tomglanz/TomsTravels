Before you go

  • How to use this guide: spots are grouped by city, then neighborhood. Tags show what kind of stop each one is. Dropping a pin on the Maps links before you travel makes it easy to see what's near your hotel.
  • Getting around: trains and subways are the way to go. Rechargeable IC cards (Suica, Pasmo, ICOCA) work on nearly all of them and in many shops, but how you get one has changed over the years. When we went, you could only add one to an iPhone wallet, not Android, and that may have changed. Research what works now so you're not stuck at the airport ticket machines like I once was. For taxis, get the Japan Taxi app as well as Uber.
  • Bring some cash. Cards are widely accepted now, but tiny restaurants, bars and market stalls are often cash-only.
  • Take the small streets. Some of my best finds were tiny places down side streets, and many only seat a handful of people.
  • Etiquette basics: keep your voice down on trains, don't tip, and carry your trash with you since public bins are rare. Try not to eat while walking in busy areas. Grab street food and eat it nearby.
  • Temples and shrines are active places of worship. Be quiet and respectful, and follow any signs about photos.
  • Taking photos in public: ask before photographing people, and be extra considerate in residential neighborhoods like Kagurazaka. Some bars and restaurants don't allow photos, so check for signs.
  • Tickets and reservations: pre-buy popular attractions like the Skytree, and book famous restaurants well ahead.
  • Things change. Some places keep limited hours, and a few of these spots may have closed or moved since my visits, so double-check on Maps before you head out.

Food words you'll see

Takoyaki
Fried octopus dumpling balls, an Osaka street food classic.
Okonomiyaki
A savory cabbage pancake cooked on a griddle. Osaka and Hiroshima each have their own style.
Yakitori / Yakiniku
Grilled chicken skewers, and grill-it-yourself meat (yakiniku).
Soba / Ramen
Buckwheat noodles, usually lighter and more traditional, and the famous noodle soup.
Izakaya
A casual pub with small plates to share.

Tokyo a million things, here are my favorite memories

Asakusa & Asakusabashi

I've spent a lot of time here every trip. It's the most touristy area, but I love it.

Sights

Senso-ji Temple

Really nice temple, plus touristy shops for gifts.

Views

Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center

A cool viewing deck and cafe up top, with a nice look over the Asakusa area. It also works as a tourist info stop. As far as I know the deck has been free, but check current details.

Shop

Tokyo Hotarudo

A tiny, hard-to-find antique shop with WWII-era antiques, kimonos and traditional wear. Pricey, but worth a look. I got an antique sake pitcher and a pin. Limited hours, so check before you go. Map

Eat

Daikokuya Tempura

Super good tempura. Read more

Dessert

Silk pudding (Asakusa Silk Pudding)

Super smooth, creamy custard-style pudding, a fun sweet stop after the temple. I went to Asakusa Silk Pudding, but similar shops are around elsewhere in Tokyo too.

Fun

Forest of Owl cafe

Fun, though there are a ton of owl cafes to choose from.

A note: if you're into animal cafes, the conversation around them has changed since my early trips. Many people now have concerns about the animals' welfare, especially for owls and other wild or nocturnal animals. Do your own research and decide what you're comfortable with.
Views

Asahi Beer Tower

As good a night view of Tokyo as the Metropolitan Building, plus a beer and a cafe on the top floor. A fraction of the Skytree price.

Views

Skytree (Sumida)

Honestly pretty cool, if pricey. Buy tickets ahead. If Fuji is out, it's awesome. Also try Tokyo Banana, a local sweet souvenir.

Shinjuku
Nightlife

Golden Gai

Worth walking through and stopping into a couple of tiny, random bars. Quiet ones often have bartenders eager to chat. Busy ones can be ruder to non-regulars. It's a maze of tiny bars with room for a handful of people each. Some charge a small cover, and some are regulars-only, so check for a sign at the door.

Fun

Robot Restaurant

As ridiculous as people say. Super touristy but somehow amazing. Unforgettable, though you don't have to go. This one may have closed or changed since 2019, so verify before planning around it.

Views

Tokyo Metropolitan Building

Great view of Tokyo, and Fuji when it's out. The observation decks have generally been free, but check current details. The neighborhood isn't otherwise amazing for me.

Walk

Shinjuku Gyoen

A big, beautiful garden right in Shinjuku, good for a calm walk and nice in cherry blossom season and fall colors. There is a small entry fee, so bring some cash or a card.

Eat

Ramen Tatsunoya (Shinjuku Otakibashi-dori)

A ramen stop in Shinjuku. Tatsunoya is typically a tonkotsu (pork-bone broth) place, so go hungry.

Shop

Kitamura Camera

Where I bought my lens. I've bought secondhand lenses here at a great deal, especially with the tax rebate system at the time. Check if that's still available. Akihabara also has great used electronics stores.

Harajuku & Meiji Jingu
Walk

Meiji Jingu

My favorite place for a walk and a great shrine. Lots of nature in a huge urban park, with cool neighborhoods nearby. Map

Eat & shop

Harajuku

High-end shopping and thrift stores. Great gyoza at Harajuku Gyoza Lou (kinda big line).

Drink

Janai Coffee

A coffee-themed speakeasy. We stopped here on our honeymoon. Great drinks and atmosphere. There's a hint in the online menu and a little trick to get in, so look it up. Map

Shibuya
Sights

Hachiko statue

A bronze statue of the loyal dog Hachiko outside Shibuya Station, and a classic meeting spot. It is fun to see at night with the giant neon screens all around, and the famous Shibuya Crossing is right nearby. There is also an Ichiran in Shibuya (see below).

Kagurazaka
Wander

The old geisha district

Charming, now residential, with tons of winding alleys and historic paths. Be quiet and respectful, since people live here.

Cafe

Manju cafe mugimaru2

Great steamed buns and tea, and a friendly cat that chills with you. It was in one of our guide books.

Tokyo Dome area & Kudanshita
Walk

Koishikawa Korakuen

A traditional Edo-period garden right by Tokyo Dome, great for walking. Especially nice for fall colors or spring. Small entry fee.

Museum

Showa-kan (National Showa Memorial Museum)

An interesting display on everyday life in Japan during and after WWII: household items, rationing, wartime homes and the hardships ordinary families faced. Small and quick to see. Check ahead for English-language info.

Akihabara

Traditional-style noodles in a cool atmosphere, near Akihabara.

Drink

Ueno
Sights

Ueno Park

Great fun for street food, and there's an old temple tucked inside the park. Beautiful to walk through, with food stands and markets at times. The Tokyo National Museum here was very good too.

Ginza & Tsukiji
Eat

Tsukiji Outer Market

The wholesale market and tuna auctions moved to Toyosu in 2018 (if you want to see auctions, look into Toyosu and book ahead). The outer market is still there, and in 2019 there was still plenty to see: very good sushi, street food and lots of shops. Still worth visiting for breakfast or a snack crawl. Go earlier in the day, since many stalls wind down by early afternoon.

Coffee

Turret Coffee

A small, popular coffee stand tucked down a side street near Tsukiji Station, a good caffeine stop on a Tsukiji visit. It is technically in Tsukiji, just outside Ginza proper. Opening hours vary by day and it has been closed some days, so check before you go.

Eat

Sukiyabashi Jiro

Famous from Jiro Dreams of Sushi. I haven't been. Very pricey and reservations needed, and it's famously hard for visitors to get one. Ginza is a very fancy neighborhood.

Everywhere
Eat

Ichiran Ramen

Vending-machine-style ordering and delicious. My favorite big chain. There's one in Shibuya and many others. We went twice on my third trip. You order from a vending machine at the entrance, then sit in a solo booth.

Eat

Yakitori on the small streets

Always take the little streets. Omoide Yokocho ("Memory Lane") near Shinjuku Station is a famous alley of tiny yakitori places.

Fun

Tokyo Disneyland

I loved it, but I'm a Disney fan in general. Tokyo Dome City also has rides and shops.

Nightlife

Unmarked bar and club buildings

There are a couple of well-known buildings that look like nothing outside but have clubs and bars inside. We ventured into a couple in Shinsaibashi. I think ours was the Misono Building (Mittera Kaikan is the other well-known one). I love my photos of the graffitied halls and staircases. Some cool artsy areas too. These are multi-floor buildings with a bunch of small bars and clubs on different floors. You can wander the halls and pop in, so it's fine to look around first.

Day trips

Scenic train

Sagano Line to Kameoka

A super scenic ride from near the bamboo forest, and I bet it's amazing in fall. We went from Torokko Arashiyama station to Umahori. In Kameoka we randomly found a phenomenal sushi place before the train back (Sushi Dokoro Sakura, I really hope it's still open!). Map This is a sightseeing train (the Sagano Scenic Railway, "Torokko"), and its schedule is seasonal, so check dates and book ahead for busy times like fall foliage. It's a detour from central Kyoto, so plan a half day.

Views

Hakone

Super cool, with amazing views of Fuji. We did a crazy day from Kyoto back to Tokyo via train, cable car, boat and bus. Fuji is often easier to see quickly from elsewhere if it's out. Guide to the full loop (Hakone Round Course) Hakone is a loop of different transport (train, cable car, boat, bus) and you can easily spend a full day on it. Look into a Hakone pass for the transport, and remember that Fuji is often hidden by clouds.
